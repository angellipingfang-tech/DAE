Standard atmosphere

Air gets colder, thinner and lighter the higher you go. The standard atmosphere is an agreed reference model of how this happens, used by aircraft and rocket designers. It is a typical average, not real weather.

Enter an altitude (height above sea level) to see the air around you at that height.

Results

Temperature
K ( °C)
How hot or cold the air is. It rises and falls in layers as you climb.
Pressure
Pa
How hard the air pushes on things. It drops quickly with height.
Air density
kg/m³
How much air is packed into each cubic meter. Thinner air gives wings and engines less to work with.
